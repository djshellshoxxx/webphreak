History and technical help

Radio identification and paging

MDC1200 and MODAT are legacy radio identification methods; the demonstration buttons generate characteristic chirps, not decodable IDs. Plectron used simultaneous tone pairs for alert receivers, while other paging formats used sequential tones. Actual assigned tone pairs and decoding rules vary.

Cassette data and PCM testing

Early microcomputers encoded data on audio cassette using frequency-shifted audio, including 1200/2400 Hz schemes. In 8 kHz sampled PCM, 1000 Hz repeats an eight-sample pattern; 1020 Hz provides more varied sample phases for testing. Browser audio cannot substitute for calibrated telecommunications measurement equipment.

Military and British trunk signaling

Secure-radio synchronization and legacy British AC-family trunk signaling are represented by simplified sound examples. Real cryptographic preambles and switch-control messages require precise framing and timing and are not implemented here.

Audio is locally synthesized and uncalibrated. Demonstration sequences are not protocol-valid unless explicitly stated.
