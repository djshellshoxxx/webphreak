Research methodology

Values came from earlier versions of this site and from user-supplied descriptions, which were treated as research leads rather than authority. Each value was checked against standards, manufacturer or regulator documents, patents, encyclopedias and historical hobbyist texts. Conflicts are kept visible rather than averaged away. Corrections and open items are listed in the research notes.

Limits of this pass. The build environment could not fetch web pages, so sources were consulted through search-engine summaries only, and no primary standard (ITU-T, Telcordia, BSTJ, ETSI, ARINC) was opened. For that reason no entry is marked “Verified”. The requested source spreadsheet could not be read either (its host was blocked), so its rows are not reconciled here.

  • ◐ Secondary sources values agree across secondary sources.
  • ⇄ Disputed sources conflict; the implemented variant and the alternative are listed.
  • ≈ Approximate simplified rendering of a real signal.
  • ? Unverified no supporting source found; kept as a research item.
  • ◇ Illustrative demonstration sound only; not a valid protocol frame, code or message.

Manuals & technical archives

  • TEXTFILES.COM — Cellphone Manuals

    Archived PDF manuals for older mobile phones, including Motorola, Nokia, Samsung, Sony Ericsson and Handspring Treo models. Useful for identifying handset features and finding original operating instructions.

  • TEXTFILES.COM — Fax Machine Manuals

    PDF manuals for fax machines, multifunction devices and some laser printers from brands such as Brother, Canon, HP, Panasonic and Sharp. A reference for operating and maintaining older office equipment.

  • TEXTFILES.COM — Telecom Manuals A–E

    The A–E section of the telephone and telecom manual archive, including 3Com, AT&T and other equipment. Browse this alphabetical collection for legacy telephone and business phone system documentation.

  • TEXTFILES.COM — Telecom Manuals F–R

    The F–R section of the telecom manual archive, with documents for Inter-Tel, Lucent, Meridian, Mitel and other systems. Includes installation, administration, maintenance and telephone user guides.

  • TEXTFILES.COM — Telecom Manuals S–Z

    The S–Z section of the telecom manual archive, including Samsung, Siemens and TIE equipment. Useful for finding legacy system descriptions, installation documentation, programming references and handset guides.

  • PBXMechanic — PBX & Telephone Manuals

    A manufacturer-organized directory of private branch exchange (PBX) manuals and business telephone guides. Covers installation, configuration, system administration and everyday phone features across legacy and newer platforms.

  • Bell System Memorial — BSPs & Technical Manuals

    A collection of Bell System Practices and technical manuals for Western Electric and related equipment. Also includes documentation for successor platforms from AT&T, Lucent and Avaya.

  • Vintage Technical Documentation Archive — Telephony

    A browsable collection organized by manufacturer and telephone company, including Bell Labs, Western Electric, Northern Telecom, AT&T and Automatic Electric. Useful for researching historical equipment and network technology.

  • Panasonic — KX-TGD630/632/633 Cordless Phone Documentation

    Official operating instructions and support material for this cordless phone family, covering setup, handset features and troubleshooting. An operating reference for compatible equipment and common problems; this is a user manual, not a board-level service schematic.

Schematics, repair & audio projects

  • TCI Library — Telephone Wiring Diagrams

    Model-organized wiring diagrams and basic schematics for vintage telephones, mostly equipment made before 1980. Useful for identifying internal connections and supporting restoration and repair.

  • British Telephones — GPO N Diagrams

    An index of historical GPO circuit and wiring diagrams for telephones, bells and private exchanges. Browse by drawing number for references on British telephone equipment; scanned drawings are available for linked entries.

  • Fairphone — Official Schematics & Repair Documentation

    Official guidance for finding model-specific phone schematics and board documentation, with links to spare-part replacement guides. Useful for understanding modular handset construction and locating manufacturer repair references.

  • PINE64 — PinePhone Schematics & Board Documentation

    Published PinePhone mainboard and USB-C daughterboard schematics, component placement drawings and component lists. Select the matching hardware revision when studying the design or investigating board-level faults.

  • Panasonic KX-TG7511/7512 — Cordless Phone Service Manual (PDF)

    Panasonic’s 2010 service manual for the Mexico variants of the KX-TG7511/7512 cordless family. Includes base-unit, handset and charger circuit schematics, PCB layouts, troubleshooting and replacement parts.

  • Holtek HT8950/HT8950A — Voice Modulator Datasheet (PDF)

    A 2006 manufacturer datasheet, hosted by SparkFun, with pin descriptions, electrical specifications and application circuit schematics. A reference for studying pitch-shifting, vibrato and robotic voice effects.

  • Adafruit — VS1053 Microphone Ogg Recorder

    A standalone audio-recording project using its own electret microphone, an Arduino and a VS1053 codec board. Includes a microphone interface circuit, wiring guidance and an example that saves recordings to an SD card.

Collections & payphones

  • Telephone Collectors International Library

    A searchable library of telephone catalogs, wiring diagrams, Bell System Practices, switching documents and historical publications. Particularly useful for identifying, researching and restoring vintage telephone equipment.

  • The Old Telephone Room

    A Canadian collection of antique and vintage telephones, payphones, prototypes and telecommunications memorabilia. Photographs, documentation and background stories highlight Bell Canada, Northern Electric, Northern Telecom and related history.

  • Dyck Telephone Collection

    Telephones and related devices from Megan and Aaron Dyck’s personal collection in Hampton, New Brunswick. A visual companion to technical archives for exploring telephone designs and collecting history.

  • PhreakNet Payphone Locations

    A community-supported map of payphone locations, with photographs and reported working status. Useful for finding surviving public telephones and contributing observations; individual reports may change over time.

  • Connections Museum

    A telecommunications museum preserving working switching equipment, telephone sets and central-office technology. Its exhibits, reference material and videos show how historical networks operated and how volunteers keep the equipment running.

  • Payphone411

    A preservation resource collecting payphone manuals, schematics, technical documents, photographs and operating information from makers such as Nortel, Protel, Elcotel and Intellicall. Useful for identifying equipment and researching vintage payphone maintenance and restoration.

  • Payphone Story

    An illustrated international encyclopaedia of payphones and telephone booths, organized by country and manufacturer. Compare designs, identify models and explore the cultural history of public telephones.

History & community archives

  • Phreaking — Wikipedia

    An introductory overview of phone phreaking, its terminology, notable figures and the evolution of telephone signaling. A starting point before exploring original documents and recordings in the historical archives.

  • The History of Phone Phreaking — Historical Documents

    Original telephone-network papers, distance-dialing and routing guides, contemporary press coverage and FBI records. Useful for studying both the engineering of the Bell System and the historical context of phone phreaking.

  • TEXTFILES.COM — Phreaking Archive

    An archive of bulletin-board-era text files documenting phone-phreaking culture and older telephone systems. A record of the community’s writing, terminology and technical interests during the early personal-computer era.

  • Phrack Magazine

    A long-running online magazine covering telecommunications, hacker culture and security research. Its earlier issues provide firsthand context for the communities that documented phone systems and the emerging computer underground.

  • Telephone World

    An illustrated guide to telephone history, switching systems, payphones and telephone sounds. Equipment explanations and historical recordings connect the machinery behind a call with what callers actually heard.

Caller ID & number lookup

  • Canada411 — Reverse Phone Lookup (Canada)

    Yellow Pages' Canadian directory includes reverse lookup for published telephone listings. A useful starting point for listed numbers in Canada; its residential directory excludes unlisted and cell-phone numbers, and some providers are not covered.

  • Cisco — Analog Caller ID Formats & FSK

    This telephone-adapter reference explains regional caller-ID formats, including Bell 202 and V.23 frequency-shift keying (FSK) and DTMF variants. Useful background on how analog caller-ID data is signaled and why phones and adapters need compatible settings.

  • CRTC — Caller ID Spoofing & STIR/SHAKEN

    The Canadian regulator's introduction to caller-ID spoofing and STIR/SHAKEN authentication for IP-based calls. Explains what authenticated caller-ID information adds and why authentication is not available on every call.

Wireless & communications research

  • Harrison’s Sandbox — GSM Security

    Articles exploring older 2G GSM networks, their security design and privacy limitations. Background for understanding how mobile-network research connects radio technology, subscriber identity and communications privacy.

  • Awesome SDR

    A curated directory of software-defined radio hardware, software, libraries, tutorials and communities. A starting point for learning the radio and signal-processing concepts behind wireless telecommunications.

  • Awesome Real-time Communications Security

    Enable Security’s collection of research, presentations, advisories and references covering VoIP, WebRTC and VoLTE security. Useful for understanding the security concerns of modern internet-based voice and video systems.

  • Packet Storm — “phreak” File Search

    A search for “phreak” within Packet Storm’s broader archive of security publications, advisories and technical research. A route to telephony-related material; the site may ask you to accept its terms before showing results.

  • GSMA — Mobile Security Algorithms

    An industry overview of algorithms used for mobile authentication, key generation, integrity protection and radio-link privacy. Places the A5 family used in 2G GSM, including legacy A5/1, in the wider development of cellular security.

  • GSMA — Security Algorithm Deployment Guidance, FS.35 (PDF · 2022)

    Version 3.0 of GSMA's guidance comparing authentication and privacy algorithms across generations of mobile networks. Background on the limitations of legacy GSM encryption and the reasons for deploying stronger standardized algorithms.

  • DECT Forum — Classic DECT Cordless Telephony

    An introduction to DECT cordless telephony, its home and business applications, and handset/base-station architecture. The security overview introduces newer authentication and encryption options and the certification programme.

  • DECT Forum — Security Certification, Steps B & C (PDF)

    An explanation of DECT's security-certification progression and the move to newer authentication and encryption. Useful for interpreting cordless-phone security claims and understanding why certification levels matter for call privacy.

Standards & modern telephony

  • ITU-T Recommendations

    The ITU’s official telecommunications standards, covering switching, signaling, transmission, telephone service and speech quality. Most finalized recommendations are available as free PDFs for readers who want the underlying specifications.

  • RFC 3261 — Session Initiation Protocol (SIP)

    The foundational specification for SIP, which establishes, modifies and ends Internet telephone calls and other multimedia sessions. The RFC Editor page also links to later updates so readers can follow the standard’s evolution.

  • Asterisk Project Documentation

    Official documentation for the Asterisk open-source communications platform, covering fundamentals, configuration, deployment, operation and development. Useful for understanding software PBXs and the relationship between modern VoIP and traditional telephone services.
