Rotary dial

Before Touch-Tone, phones dialed by interrupting the line current: dialing 5 sends five clicks. Drag a finger hole clockwise to the metal finger stop and let go. If you let go early, nothing is dialed. You can also tap a hole, or focus it and press Enter, to dial it automatically.

History and help

Touch-Tone

Dual-tone multi-frequency (DTMF) signalling replaced rotary-dial pulses on subscriber lines from the 1960s. Each key sends one tone from a low group and one from a high group; the combination is internationally standardized in ITU-T Q.23 (ITU-T Q.23; overview: Wikipedia).

The fourth column and AUTOVON

The 1633 Hz column (A, B, C, D) is missing from ordinary phones. On the US military AUTOVON network those keys selected call precedence: A Flash Override, B Flash, C Immediate, D Priority (ATIS glossary, Wikipedia: Autovon). They are still DTMF subscriber tones; they are not the MF trunk tones on the Bluebox / MF page, which use a different 700–1700 Hz frequency set.

Evidence status: the frequency matrix is consistent across secondary sources; ITU-T Q.23 itself was not opened in this research pass, so entries are marked “secondary”.
