History and help

Radio paging and identification

Fire and EMS paging used sequential two-tone formats such as Motorola Quick Call II; Plectron receivers alerted volunteer firefighters (BatLabs: Quick Call II). Aircraft are called on shared HF frequencies with four-tone SELCAL codes (ASRI SELCAL guide). MDC1200 and MODAT identified two-way radios with short data bursts.

Cassette data

The Kansas City standard came out of a 1975 BYTE symposium: 300 bit/s, with “0” as four cycles of 1200 Hz and “1” as eight cycles of 2400 Hz (BYTE, Feb. 1976; Wikipedia). Processor Technology’s CUTS added a 1200 bit/s mode. The Tarbell interface used a different phase-encoded scheme at about 1500 bit/s; it is not synthesized here because its manual could not be consulted (Tarbell manual scan).

Test tones and calibration

A 1000 Hz tone is an exact sub-multiple of the 8 kHz PCM sampling rate, which can bias digital level measurements; CCITT recommended 1020 Hz for digital tests and North American practice used 1004 Hz (CCITT O.6 text). Telephone “0 dBm” means one milliwatt into a 600 Ω line. Your browser and speakers are not calibrated, so these files are reference frequencies, not reference levels.

EAS/SAME: the attention tones and AFSK parameters follow 47 CFR 11.31 (LII); no alert header is generated. VINSON: no official description of its sync sound is public; the key is a generic chirp.
