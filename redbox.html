History: red boxes, coin signalling and the decline of payphones

What a red box was

Payphones on ACTS-equipped lines reported deposited coins by playing coin tones down the line. A “red box” was a device that played the same tones, so it only worked where the network trusted in-band audio from the handset (Wikipedia: Red box). This page reproduces the sounds for listening only.

Why it stopped working

Secondary sources describe several changes: ACTS was phased out, carriers added filtering, and “smart” payphones moved coin validation and rating into the phone itself. Nortel’s Millennium payphones, for example, validate coins with an electronic coin mechanism in the set and do not use ACTS coin tones (Wikipedia: Nortel payphones; TelephoneWorld). No single model ended the technique everywhere; networks changed at different times.

Payphones today

US payphone numbers fell from roughly two million in the late 1990s to under about 100,000 by the mid-2010s, and in 2018 the FCC removed some payphone audit rules because so few remained (Axios, 2017; FCC 18-21). Some payphones still operate. These counts come from secondary reporting; exact FCC figures were not verified.
